## Intro
I think most people have seen HuggingFace and all the drama around AI safety recently and are broadly worried about AI, with fears ranging from existential risk, future stability, power concentration, societal disruption, etc. AI is getting better at literally everything and unfortunately we have look ahead to a rapidly changing society, and be aware that this intelligence may not be completely aligned.\
\
Most people, however, are also not safety researchers, but still are concerned. It seems to me there is a widespread/general hopelessness that they can do anything meaningful or contribute to the problem. Here is the crux of the problem: many people still do not know what they can do. Here, I suggest that communication is more effective than you think.
## Currently
Current AIS opportunities are also too high commitment, ranging from barrier to entry (require preexisting research or other reqs) or time (need to learn a lot of stuff really fast). As for the low-commitment work, of course, there is the classic "talk to your representative" or "having conversations," but many people tell me these feel not very substantive and seem rather weak in the face of existential risk. Additionally, most people don't want to talk about dying: I'd much rather talk about basketball or getting boba rather than the pessimistic "hey, we all might go extinct." So I will discuss other methods on how to "talk to other people" but more effectively.
## Change your mindset!
![Change Your Mindset](/images/change-your-mindset.jpg)

I think a lot of people are on the side of some kind of skepticism about this being real ("it's just numbers and matrices and a better autocomplete") and helpless fatalism ("we're all going to die, and if we don't, we're all going to be homeless"). But what this does is that it traps you in a state of mind that prevents action. After all, an idiot in motion goes further than a genius at rest (not saying that you are an idiot). The end result for either is the same: nobody does anything, labs face zero accountability for pursuing aggressive improvement without caring about alignment. \
\
I think we should all be of the mindset that yes, this is indeed a concrete problem and we should care about labs pursuing alignment and good practice, and we should care about the right policies being passed in government; and that yes, while ex-risk and all of the above are possible, every individual in the human race has more agency than they think and we are capable of solving great problems. This shift grounds us in actual demands: it is hard to "prevent AGI." It is easier to ask for stress-testing, audits, and actionable outcomes. We need accountability for AI, just like plane crashes, and a mindset away from cynicism or fatalism can help with that.\
\
Being aware of how other people can rabbit-hole themselves into this mindset can also help with how you frame your discussions. Your goal shouldn't to make someone afraid, it should be making risks credible, what remains uncertain, what we should do, and what we want in response. \
\
For example, with skeptics, it's probably easier to argue "models can lie" than "models will cause XYZ extinction scenario." Examining the links inbetween can lead to something more productive, such as highlighting what's been observed (HuggingFace, for example), what can be inferred (swarms can hack on the internet against our instructions), what would have to happen for consequences to follow (we don't take it seriously and ignore it). Discussing conditions such as access; control allows for more disagreement and more effective conversations. Personally, I like to ask what evidence would warrant their concern. \
\
Another concern is that "they are playing these fears for the IPO." I personally believe that there is no trick and that researchers are genuinely terrified of the AIs they are working on, because money is not an issue in the grave. Even so, a company benefitting from their own warning does not prove that the warning is false. Assessing the evidence separately from the messaging also helps; and is also more reason why we should have push for better regulation, transparency, and safeguards in these frontier labs. \
\
Also, I just want to mention. For those who feel powerless and feel like they just have to hope the frontier labs figure it out: actions are worth taking if it improves our chances, even if we cannot guarantee the outcome. For me, if misaligned AGI destroyed us, I would feel comfortable knowing I did what I could rather than passively accepting it. I acknowledge the scale of this problem, but many little steps combined can turn into something great. This is how change happened in the past, this is how it can happen today.      
## Stay educated and talk to people effectively
Being able to know the details is a very good asset! Understanding what is going on at a high level will 1. help you understand and not feel as helpless 2. elucidate harder concepts to other people! A lightweight learning routine, starting around basics (depending on your level of expertise) and building up to being understand research or updates from OpenAI or Anthropic's blog does not take as much effort as it seems. There are numerous, numerous YouTube videos; podcasts on how they work and can help out. I encourage reading past the headline and looking at details as well. \
\
Now I know I previously said "having conversations" isn't very substantive, but I think there is a way to do it properly to get people to care/take action. Often times what I see is that the conversation ends with knowing more, but not an inspiration to act. Talk about the concrete issue, discuss it, share your observations, insecurities, fears on that incident, and ask questions on the other person's view! Safety-pilling people isn't about scaring the other person that we're all going to die, but introducing a concern that may affect their life and what they care about. You would be surprised how many people will buy into your concern if you know your facts, explain observations, listen to concerns, and ask for what would help! Ending with suggesting a specific action, such as VOTING :), attending public meetings, talking to other people, reading policy, etc. It also shouldn't be an obligation for them, but if they do care, a right push in the right direction is often times what it needs. Many people still do not know about safety, and we can change that.
## Voice is power
The bottom line is that communication is effective. Only about a couple hundred thousand have full agentic workflows, then couple hundred millions use LLMs, more people beyond that think it's a better Google search and billions probably still don't know it exists. In your community, you can talk to people about this! Knowledge about safety is something that should be more widespread. Raising awareness is really underrated :), and doing it effectively will have greater results than you expect.\
\
Also go vote, and make civic requests!
